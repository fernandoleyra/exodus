IMF terms for published statistical Data
========================================

IMF content is published "All Rights Reserved" and its general terms forbid
redistribution and derivative works. Its published statistical Data are carved
out of that prohibition by special terms, quoted below from
https://www.imf.org/en/_site_imf/about/copyright-and-terms (effective
11 October 2024) as retrieved on 2026-09-19. The canonical
https://www.imf.org/external/terms.htm returns HTTP 403 to non-browser clients;
the URL above serves the same notice with HTTP 200.

Scope. "Data" is defined to include "Most statistical data available on
www.IMF.org, www.data.IMF.org, or the iData Portal that explicitly identify the
International Monetary Fund as the source", and names the World Economic Outlook
database explicitly. The DataMapper API, from which Exodus reads, serves the WEO
database.

The grant:

  "Notwithstanding the general prohibition on the commercial use of IMF Content,
  with respect to published statistical data made available on IMF Sites, the
  following special terms shall govern. [...] You may download, extract, copy,
  create derivative works, publish, distribute, and use Data obtained from IMF
  Sites, subject to the following conditions:

    - Whether obtained directly from the IMF or another party, when Data is
      distributed or reproduced in any manner, it must appear accurately with
      attribution to the IMF as the source, e.g. 'Source: International Monetary
      Fund, Database Name, <<link to the dataset>>.'

    - Users shall not infringe upon the integrity of the Data and in particular
      shall refrain from any act of alteration of the Data that intentionally
      affects its nature or accuracy. If the Data is materially transformed by
      the User, this must be stated explicitly along with the required source
      citation.

    - Users who make IMF Data available to other Users through any type of
      distribution or download environment agree to take reasonable efforts to
      communicate and promote compliance by their users with these terms.

    - If IMF Data is sold by Users as a standalone product, sellers must inform
      purchasers that the Data is available free of charge from the IMF. [...]"

Two limits that remain in force
-------------------------------

The general terms state that "The IMF prohibits the bulk download of information
by automated technology without explicit permission." Exodus reads three
indicator series once and caches them; it does not mirror the database.

The special terms close with "For any potential commercial reuse of IMF Data,
please email copyright@imf.org to request permission." This sits awkwardly beside
a grant that opens by setting aside the prohibition on commercial use. Exodus is
a non-commercial open-source project, so the tension does not arise here — but
anyone reusing this repository's snapshot commercially should resolve it with the
IMF rather than rely on this file.

How Exodus satisfies these
--------------------------

Attribution in the required form is carried on /#/sources and beside every IMF
figure on /#/concordance.

The transformation is material and is stated explicitly in the same place:
regional aggregates are removed, the population series is rescaled from millions
to people, and a single year is selected from a database that extends into
projection years.
